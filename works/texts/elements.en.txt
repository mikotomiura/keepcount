Water takes the shape of what holds it. Poured into a bowl, it is round; spilled on a table, it spreads thin and finds the edge.

Fire needs air and something to burn. It moves upward, it flickers, and it leaves less than it found.

Earth stays. Stones are the slowest things we touch, and a field remembers the plough for years.

Air is everywhere and seen nowhere. We know it by what it moves: a curtain, a flag, the surface of a pond.

Old books named four elements, some named five. None of them is a substance in the modern sense, but each is still a way of saying how things behave.
